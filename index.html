How we plan

  1. Set the goal

    Net new accounts, named accounts or both. The goal decides the format.

  2. Check the fit

    Research with business units against our ICP and business requirements.

  3. Choose the format

    Attend or exhibit, based on audience, cost and goal.

  4. Approve and budget

    Sign-off before any spend. Early-bird pricing and multi-event bundling keep costs down.

  5. Plan with the teams

    SDR, Sales, Finance, Creative and Social Media. SOPs and a SharePoint event hub keep every phase consistent.

Case studies

Three programs where the approach changed from one year to the next, and what I changed.

Tech Xchange

Fulcrum Digital's annual B2B summit for Insurance, BFSI and AI leaders

Owned end to end

Why we run Tech Xchange

Tech Xchange is our own annual summit, and we use it to build trust with our existing customers and to win net-new accounts. When prospects see us in the room with customers we already work with, they see proof, and that leads to work. For our existing customers, a summit like this is also where we close bottom-of-funnel projects with them. C-suite leaders across Insurance, BFSI and AI come together in one room, with time to talk.

Challenge

In year one the full program had to come together in 5 weeks.

What I changed

  • Planned further ahead, using what we learned in the first edition.
  • Introduced Cvent for registration and badge printing, for a smoother check-in.
  • Introduced HubSpot for promotions and outreach.

Outcome

Pipeline contribution has grown across 3+ consecutive years, with 250+ C-suite attendees per event.

UCISA

UK higher education technology conference

Swag strategy

Context

UCISA brings university IT and digital leaders together. We exhibit there to reach higher education decision-makers, so a busy booth matters.

Challenge

For two years in a row, we struggled with footfall at the booth. Our giveaways were not giving people a reason to stop and talk, and the team on the booth wasn’t working from the same picture of who would be there.

What I did

  • Opened a two-way conversation with Sales. I asked the sales team for their input and suggestions, including those who would be on-site and those handling the post-event follow-up.
  • Understood the event and the audience. Together we looked at who attends UCISA, which clients we wanted to meet, and what has traditionally worked at this event.
  • Chose swag to fit the audience. Based on that, we moved to premium chocolates and unique water bottles, picked for these visitors rather than for the usual trade-show tote.
  • Planned the post-event experience. Sales and Marketing agreed in advance how booth conversations would be followed up.

Why it worked

The people who talk to clients every day knew what would catch their attention. Better communication with Sales, combined with research on the event and its attendees, turned giveaways from a cost item into a way to start conversations.

Takeaway

Marketing choices for an event are strongest when the sales team helps make them, and when they come from knowing the audience instead of repeating last year’s plan.

ITC Vegas

The flagship insurance technology event. Insurance is our bread and butter.

Booth growth

Challenge

We started with only a shell-scheme stand, which was not enough to carry demos for our most important vertical.

What I changed

  • Booth set-up. We started in 2024 with only a shell-scheme stand and upgraded step by step. By 2026 we had added an LED display and demo tables.
  • Better SDR outreach. We worked with the SDR team on targeted outreach before the show, aimed at key named accounts.

Outcome

The improved set-up and the sharper SDR outreach helped us close a key named account.
